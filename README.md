## 📊 Task Workflow

### Step 1: Descriptive Statistics and Univariate Analysis

The first step focuses on understanding the structure and characteristics of the dataset.

Activities:
- Load and inspect the dataset.
- Examine rows, columns, and data types.
- Calculate descriptive statistics for numerical variables.
- Analyze categorical variables and their frequencies.
- Check missing values and duplicate records.
- Create histograms and bar charts to understand data distributions.

**Expected Outcome:** A clear understanding of the dataset, its quality, and the distribution of individual variables.

### Step 2: SQL for Business Questions

SQL is used to extract information and answer business-related questions through filtering, aggregation, grouping, and joins.

Example business questions:

1. What are the top five products by revenue during the last six months?
2. What is the monthly sales or user acquisition trend?
3. Which product categories generate the highest revenue?
4. Which regions or cities contribute the most to total sales?
5. What is the average order value?
6. Which customer segments contribute the most revenue?
7. How do sales and revenue change over time?

**SQL Concepts:**
- SELECT and WHERE
- GROUP BY and ORDER BY
- Aggregate functions such as SUM(), COUNT(), and AVG()
- Date filtering
- JOIN operations when multiple tables are available

**Expected Outcome:** Business insights derived from SQL queries and aggregated data.

*Note: Business questions must be adapted to the available dataset and its columns. Questions that cannot be answered using the available data should be documented accordingly.*

### Step 3: Multivariate Analysis and Correlation

This step explores relationships between multiple variables to identify patterns and potential associations.

Activities:
- Create scatter plots to compare numerical variables.
- Generate correlation heatmaps for numerical features.
- Use pair plots to examine relationships between multiple numerical variables.
- Explore relationships between relevant business variables.

Example analyses:
- Marketing expenditure versus sales
- Product price versus quantity sold
- Customer age versus purchasing behavior

**Expected Outcome:** Better understanding of relationships, correlations, and patterns within the dataset.

*Correlation does not necessarily imply causation.*

### Step 4: Static Dashboard Mockup

Design a static dashboard mockup using Excel, PowerPoint, or Google Slides based on the findings from EDA and business analysis.

Potential KPIs:
- Total Revenue
- Total Sales or Orders
- Average Order Value
- Monthly Revenue Trend
- Top-Performing Products
- Category-Wise Sales
- Region-Wise Performance

The dashboard should present important metrics and visualizations in a clear, organized, and business-friendly format.

**Expected Outcome:** A dashboard design that communicates key business metrics and supports data-driven decision-making.
